Questions

Credible — Is the result true?

C01AI-armed seniorComplicatedundermine●●●●●

Rd isn't a real comparator anymore — VRd already beat it in SWOG S0777. Didn't MAIA just beat a straw man?

First principleA randomized trial answers 'better than this comparator'; whether that matters today depends on whether the comparator was standard when patients were enrolled and how it links to today's standard.
30-second answer
It depends on which year you mean. MAIA enrolled from 2015 to 2017, when Rd was the label standard for transplant-ineligible patients; S0777 was published in 2017 and also enrolled patients with transplant intent. So Rd was a fair control then. Today the honest claim is narrower: D-Rd beats Rd. Against VRd we only have an Rd-anchored indirect comparison favouring D-Rd, HR 0.59 — hypothesis-level. The direct triplet-versus-quadruplet answer comes from CEPHEUS and IMROZ, not MAIA.
Follow-ups

Is an indirect comparison worth anything?
It adjusts for measured differences only. Era, subsequent therapy and assessment schedules differ. I treat it as consistent with, not proof of.

Then why not just give VRd?
For a fit patient I'd add bortezomib to the antibody backbone rather than choose between them — that's what CEPHEUS and IMROZ tested.

What the AI told them
MAIA compared D-Rd with Rd, a standard at the time. Since VRd has shown superiority over Rd, many experts consider the comparator outdated.
Blind spot
No date logic: MAIA enrolled 2015-17 and S0777 was published in 2017, with transplant-intent patients included. No mention that the only D-Rd vs VRd evidence is an Rd-anchored indirect comparison, or that the quadruplet trials compared against VRd, not D-Rd.

C02StatisticianClear●●●●○

The 2021 OS result came from an interim analysis. Are we over-reading an early look?

First principleAn interim analysis spends alpha against a stricter prespecified boundary; a result that crosses it is valid, and later updates are descriptive.
30-second answer
The data are clear: the interim OS analysis crossed its prespecified stopping boundary — p = 0.0013 against a boundary of 0.0414, HR 0.68. Everything after that, including the final analysis at 89 months, is descriptive follow-up of a result already established. And the HR didn't drift: 0.66 at 64.5 months, 0.67 at final. A lucky early estimate usually shrinks; this one didn't.
Follow-ups

Why does a stable HR matter?
Random highs regress toward the truth with more events. Stability across three analyses argues the early effect was real.

C03AI-armed seniorClearrebut●●●●○

It was open-label. Isn't the PFS benefit biased?

First principleAssessment bias affects endpoints that require judgment; centrally assessed endpoints and death resist it.
30-second answer
Fair concern for subjective endpoints. Two facts answer it: PFS in MAIA was centrally assessed, and overall survival — which an assessor cannot bias — went the same direction, HR 0.67 at final. When the unbiasable endpoint agrees, assessment bias isn't the explanation.
Follow-ups

Could open-label affect treatment decisions, like earlier discontinuation in the control arm?
It could affect behaviour. Time to next treatment was also longer with D-Rd, HR 0.51, which is hard to produce through behaviour alone.

What the AI told them
Open-label designs can bias progression-based endpoints, which is a recognized limitation of MAIA.
Blind spot
Omits that MAIA's PFS was centrally assessed and that OS — which no assessor can bias — moved the same way.

C04StatisticianComplicatedundercut●●●●●⚠ UNVERIFIED

OS depends on what control patients received at relapse. How many Rd patients later got an anti-CD38 antibody?

First principleOS compares whole strategies including post-progression therapy; limited access to the experimental class in the control arm makes early use look better than use at any time.
30-second answer
Right — OS here compares 'anti-CD38 up front' with 'Rd first, then what was available'. The exact proportion of Rd patients who later received an anti-CD38 should be quoted from the final analysis. What supports the result: time to next treatment was much longer, HR 0.51, and the survival gain held through seven years. So I phrase the claim as: using daratumumab first, under that era's access, prolongs survival — not that timing never matters.

So saving daratumumab for relapse could be just as good?
That's a sequencing question nobody has randomized. In older patients, attrition means many never reach second line — that's the bet you make by waiting.

C07StatisticianComplicated●●○○○

Is MRD negativity a validated surrogate for overall survival in this setting?

First principleA surrogate is validated when treatment effects on it predict treatment effects on survival across trials, not just when MRD-negative patients do better.
30-second answer
At the patient level MRD negativity strongly predicts longer PFS; trial-level validation for OS is the harder bar. That's why I don't lean on MRD for MAIA — MAIA doesn't need a surrogate, it has final OS. Where MRD matters is the quadruplet trials, whose OS is still immature.

C08AI-armed seniorChaoticrebut●●●●○

Serious adverse events were higher with daratumumab. So it causes more harm while 'improving' survival?

First principleOverall survival counts every death, toxic or not; a positive OS difference already includes the harm.
30-second answer
Let me take the data part first. Serious adverse events were higher, 77% versus 70%, and deaths due to adverse events at the final analysis were 12% versus 11% — similar. Overall survival counts every death, and it favours D-Rd: 53% versus 39% alive at seven years. So the net is clearly positive. The extra harm is real and mostly infection, which is why prophylaxis and early vigilance matter.
Follow-ups

What kind of deaths?
Infection is the main excess: in the pooled MAIA and ALCYONE analysis, grade 5 infections were 3.0% versus 1.5%.

What the AI told them
D-Rd was associated with more serious adverse events, particularly neutropenia and pneumonia, which should be weighed against the efficacy benefit.
Blind spot
Misses that OS already nets out toxic deaths, and that deaths due to adverse events were similar at final (12% vs 11%).

C10AI-armed seniorComplicated●●●○○⚠ UNVERIFIED

Daratumumab shows up on SPEP. Couldn't response or progression be misclassified?

First principleDaratumumab is itself an IgG-κ monoclonal antibody; on SPEP/IFE it can mimic residual M-protein, so it hides responses rather than creating them.
30-second answer
The interference is real — the label says daratumumab can be detected on SPEP and IFE in IgG-kappa patients. The direction matters: it can make a true complete response look like residual disease. That biases against D-Rd, not for it. In practice, a daratumumab-specific IFE reflex assay separates the drug from the disease.
What the AI told them
Daratumumab can interfere with SPEP and immunofixation, potentially affecting response assessment in IgG-kappa myeloma.
Blind spot
Doesn't say which direction: the interference makes a true CR look like residual disease — a bias against D-Rd, not for it.

Applicable — Does it apply to my patient?

A01AI-armed seniorComplicatedundermine●●●●●⚠ UNVERIFIED

Only a tiny fraction of MAIA patients were Asian. Why should this apply to Taiwanese patients?

First principleThe efficacy of a target-directed antibody transfers when the target and disease biology are the same; tolerability depends on body size, comorbidity and infection exposure, which differ more between populations.
30-second answer
Fair: the label reports 1% Asian patients in MAIA, so MAIA alone cannot answer this. I split the question. Efficacy depends on CD38 biology, which isn't ethnic. Tolerability depends on body weight, frailty and infection exposure — and those I can adjust: dexamethasone 20 mg for BMI under 18.5, zoster prophylaxis, vigilance for infection in the first six months. So I extrapolate the efficacy and personalise the safety.
Follow-ups

Any Asian data with daratumumab at all?
Asian-enrolling daratumumab trials and regional real-world series exist; have the specific citation ready.

What the AI told them
Asian patients were under-represented in MAIA, but daratumumab efficacy has generally been consistent across ethnic groups, so results are considered applicable.
Blind spot
Gives no number (1% Asian per the US label) and does not separate efficacy, which depends on biology, from tolerability, which depends on body size, frailty and care setting.

A02Community oncologistComplicated●●●●●

My patient is 84 and frail. D-Rd, or something gentler?

First principleFitness, not age, predicts tolerance; dexamethasone contributes much of the toxicity in frail patients, so removing it can keep the antibody's benefit at lower cost.
30-second answer
It depends on frailty, not age. In MAIA's frail subgroup PFS favoured D-Rd, HR 0.64, but the OS confidence interval crossed 1. The better evidence for the truly frail is IFM2017-03: median age 81, daratumumab-lenalidomide with dexamethasone only in the first two cycles beat Rd — median PFS 53.4 versus 22.5 months. For my frail 84-year-old, that's DR, dexamethasone-sparing. IFM 2026 says the same.
Follow-ups

What did IFM2017-03 cost in toxicity?
More grade 3-5 neutropenia, 55% vs 24%, but infections were similar, 19% vs 21%.

Frail patients had an OS HR of 0.79 with the CI crossing 1. So no survival benefit in the frail?

A04Community oncologistComplicated●●●●○

High-risk cytogenetics — is D-Rd enough?

First principleA consistent relative effect on a worse baseline still leaves a worse absolute outcome; high-risk disease is where deeper regimens have the most room to help.
30-second answer
The relative benefit holds — PFS HR 0.59 in high-risk cytogenetics. But high-risk patients still do worse in absolute terms. In a fit high-risk patient I'd consider a quadruplet — D-VRd or Isa-VRd — where accessible. The isolated gain(1q) HR of 0.36 is a small post hoc subgroup; I wouldn't build a decision on it.

CrCl is 25. Can I still use D-Rd?

First principleMonoclonal antibodies are cleared by catabolism, not the kidney; lenalidomide is about 82% excreted unchanged in urine, so only lenalidomide needs adjusting.

Yes — adjust lenalidomide, not daratumumab. Label: CrCl under 30 not on dialysis, lenalidomide 15 mg every other day; on dialysis, 5 mg daily, after dialysis on dialysis days. Daratumumab's population PK showed no meaningful effect of CrCl 15 to 89. One caveat: patients this impaired were likely outside MAIA's eligibility, so efficacy here is extrapolated.
Follow-ups

Will the dose go back up if renal function recovers?
Yes — re-dose to the new CrCl band; myeloma kidney often improves with response.

How common are infusion reactions, and how do I run the first infusion?

30-second answer
Infusion reactions occurred in 37% at the first IV infusion, 2% at the second and 6% cumulatively after that. First dose: 1,000 mL, start at 50 mL/h, increase by 50 mL/h every hour to a maximum of 200 mL/h — or split 8 mg/kg over days 1 and 2.
