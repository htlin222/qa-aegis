# The five questioners

Each persona is a lens that produces questions the others miss. Tag every question with the person most likely to ask it.

| id | Who | What they probe | How they ask | What disarms them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ai_senior` | The AI-armed senior. Asked an AI tool during your talk and is checking you against it. | Headline numbers, guideline status, "isn't X now preferred?" | Confident, often a yes/no trap built on a relative effect or a guideline line | Agree with what the AI gets right in one sentence, then add the thing it cannot know: trial detail, absolute numbers, local rules, data newer than its training |
| `statistician` | Methods-minded colleague or reviewer | Surrogates, censoring, interim analyses, multiplicity, indirect comparisons | Precise, technical | Name the statistical principle first, then the number |
| `community` | Community oncologist / hematologist who will actually prescribe | Who to give it to, logistics, what to do on Monday | Practical, case-based ("my 82-year-old with…") | A decision rule and the one test that changes it |
| `pharmacist` | Clinical pharmacist | Dose, renal/hepatic adjustment, interactions, premeds, stability, lab interference | Specific, numeric | Label section and the number; never approximate |
| `payer` | NHI reviewer, administrator, or anyone asking "who pays" | Reimbursement status, caps, prior authorization, cost per QALY, what happens when the cap runs out | Blunt | The current rule with its effective date, and what you do inside it |

## The `ai_senior` drill

1. Write the AI answer they are holding (`ai_answer`), in the AI's register: tidy, relative, guideline-level.
2. Find where it is thin (`blind_spot`). The usual places:
   - relative effect quoted without the absolute difference
   - control arm not compared with today's standard
   - excluded or under-represented populations (e.g. Asian proportion)
   - local approval and reimbursement
   - data published after the model's training cutoff
   - practice details: dose adjustments, lab interference, prophylaxis
3. Your answer acknowledges the correct part in one clause, then moves to the blind spot. Never mock the tool or the asker; the room sides with whoever looks fair.
