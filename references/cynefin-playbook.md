# Cynefin playbook for Q&A

Cynefin sorts problems by the relationship between cause and effect. In Q&A it sorts questions by **what kind of answer is honest**, and so which opener to use.

| Domain | Test | Honest answer type | Opener | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clear` | One number, label line or rule answers it; experts would not disagree | State it, cite it | "The data are clear: …" / "The label is specific: …" | Over-explaining; hedging a settled fact |
| `complicated` | Answerable, but the answer depends on a patient or context variable that expertise can resolve | Decision rule: "if X then A, if Y then B" | "It depends on X — here's how I decide…" | Giving one answer to a question that has two |
| `complex` | No trial answers it; outcomes only knowable in retrospect | Admit the gap, give your reasoning, name the trial that would settle it | "We don't have data for that. My reasoning is…, and the trial that would answer it is…" | Faking certainty; refusing to reason at all |
| `chaotic` | Ambush: hostile tone, several questions at once, a premise that is wrong, or a personal challenge | Stabilize first: take the answerable part, correct the premise calmly, defer the rest | "Let me take the part I can answer with data first…" | Answering the emotion; defending everything at once |
| `confused` | You cannot tell what is being asked | Restate and confirm before answering | "If I understand correctly, you're asking… — is that right?" | Answering the question you wish they had asked |

## Moves between domains

- A `chaotic` question usually contains a `clear` core. Find it, answer it, and the rest often dissolves.
- A `complicated` question asked by the `ai_senior` is often framed as `clear` ("isn't D-VRd simply better?"). Reframing it to `complicated` is the answer: name the variable.
- A `complex` question is not a failure. Saying "nobody knows; here is how I'd think about it" is the most credible sentence in Q&A, as long as it is followed by reasoning.

## Timing

30 seconds per answer. One number per answer unless asked for more. End on the patient, not on the statistic.
